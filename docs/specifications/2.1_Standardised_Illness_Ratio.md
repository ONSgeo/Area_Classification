# Standardised Illness Ratio

## 1.0 Introduction
This specificaiton covers the calculation of the Standardised Illness Ratio (SIR) in the area classification pipeline.

SIR was previously employed in the 2001 and 2011 Area Classifications as age was recognised as a key factor in determining prevalence of health issues and disability. The SIR is a measure of the number of people with a disability or long-term illness in an area, standardised against expected numbers, given the age distribution of an area and the national age-disaggregated disability/illness rates. We have continued the use of this calculation, following the interim [2021 output area classifications for England and Wales](https://data.geods.ac.uk/dataset/output-area-classification-2021).

The national average SIR is 100, so values for each area will be relative to this. For example an area with value of 90 means it has a 10% lower illness rate than the national average, whilst an area with a value of 110 has a 10% higher illness rate. 

## 2.0 Assumptions and requirements
As the SIR is a relative indicator, a national average value for the UK is needed which requires data for all countries - England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales. In this sense, we are assuming it is appropriate to combine the 2022 data from Scotland (UV303a.csv), with 2021 data from England and Wales ('disabilitycensus2021.xlsx') and Northern Ireland ('ni_downloads/census-2021-ms-d02.xlsx').

## 4.0 Method
The calculation of SIR has been conducted in line with the formula used by [Wyszomierski, 2023](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10189266/2/THESIS_Jakub_Jan_Wyszomierski.pdf):

In this work, the national proportion (`nat_prop`) is calculated by summing the LAD level disability count and overall population data into UK-level values. 
- <img width="15" height="24" alt="SIR_equation_pia" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8e165d49-b35d-4d53-a8f4-2efc3b7f4708" />  is the population size of an age group 𝑎 in area 𝑖.

- <img width="65" height="22" alt="SIR_equation_Earnapia" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/735b424e-2a90-473d-9005-eb29c08e6adc" /> is the expected ill for a given age group α and a given geography 𝑖. This is an estimation of the number of people in an area who are expected to have a long-term illness or disability, assuming the illness proportions in the area are equal to the UK national average. The expected ill value (`exp_ill`) is calculated by multiplying the proportion of the UK’s population that have an disability or long-term illness for each age range (‘<15 and >=65’ years and ‘15-64’ years in the code) by the total population of each age range in each LAD.  

### 4.1 Steps to achieve this: 
1. Each local authority is split into data for ‘<15 and >=65’ and ‘15-64’. 
2. The national proportions (`nat_prop`). This is done by two steps:
- a. group by the two age groups and sum total population and total disabled
- b. calculate the national proportion is calculated by dividing the disability count for a given age group by the population for a given age group
3. The expected ill (`exp_ill`) is calculated by multiplying the (`nat_prop`) with the total population for each LAD. At this stage there will still be two rows for every LAD. One for ‘<15 and >=65’ and ‘15-64’.
4. Next calculate `exp_ill_all` by summing the two `exp_ill` for each LAD area. So there is now only one row for each LAD.
5. Then calculate the standard illness ratio by dividing the disability count (step 2a) `disability_count` by the expected illness `exp_ill_sum` (step 4) and times by 100.

### 4.2 Strengths
SIR provides a single measure of long-term illness and disability in an area. The SIR replaced the census disability indicator as areas with a higher proportion of older people can be expected to be characterised by higher disability rates, but the SIR accounts for these age groups by looking at the data in two different age bands ‘Aged 0 to 16 and 64 and over’ and ‘Aged 16 to 64’.

### 4.3 Limitations
Local Authority Districts (LADs) can vary in size and may not be entirely homogeneous so the SIR must be interpreted as a broad relative measure.

Previously, [Wyszomierski (2023)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10189266/2/THESIS_Jakub_Jan_Wyszomierski.pdf) used age bands consisting of ‘Aged 0 to 16 and 64 and over’ and ‘Aged 16 to 64’ to calculate the SIR for the interim 2021 OAC England and Wales. However since more data has been made available. The Scotland and Northern Ireland data which has been published is in age band which differ from each other and the bands previously used, so it was not possible to use the age bands used in the interim solution. As we require consistent age bands across all datasets to enable cross-tabulation, we have used "Under 15 and 65 or older", and "15 to 64" (‘<15 and >=65’ and ‘15-64’). 

Note: [Gale (2014)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1446924/) constructed SIR for the 2011 OAC, using ‘Aged 0 to 15 and 65 and over’ and ‘Aged 16 to 64’.
